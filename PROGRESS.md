clearly-flagged next step -- not as a system already proven on real pests.

### Prompt 5 Step 4 -- compression delta (a striking number that turned out to be a bug)

Ran `R/07_compression_delta.R` for the first time. First result: compressed
86.9% vs. uncompressed 71.0% -- a -15.9% delta, which would have been a
genuinely interesting "compression costs accuracy" finding. Didn't take it
at face value: the run also logged "184 training rows dropped for
non-finite features" for the uncompressed condition vs. only 8 for
compressed, a 20x disparity worth explaining before trusting the headline
number. Traced it to `harmonic_energy_ratio_from_spectrum()` (in
`pkg/whatsappaudioR/R/wa_features.R`) returning `NA` whenever a window had
no well-defined peak frequency (near-silent windows) -- and two long real
recordings had far more such windows in the uncompressed condition
specifically (genuine silence stays silent; WhatsApp's own lossy
noise-shaping incidentally raises the noise floor just enough to dodge a
literal zero-Hz peak). Net effect: the uncompressed model's test accuracy
was computed on 100 usable windows out of ~217, vs. 221 of ~222 for
compressed -- not a fair comparison at all, just very different amounts of
usable test data. Fixed `harmonic_energy_ratio_from_spectrum()` to return 0
(a legitimate value, not "unknown") in that case, added a regression test,
reinstalled the package, and re-ran everything. **The delta collapsed to
-0.4%** (86.9% compressed vs. 86.6% uncompressed) -- no detectable
accuracy cost from WhatsApp's compression on these classes. Full
interpretation, including the honest account of chasing down the -15.9%
artifact, in `docs/compression_delta.md`.

## What we can and cannot claim (Prompt 5, Step 6)

### 1. Claims fully supported by real-audio measurement

- **Real Orthoptera stridulation survives WhatsApp's 16kHz/16kbps Opus
  compression well.** Measured directly on real iNaturalist recordings of
  *Chorthippus biguttulus* (survivability 0.986) and *Chorthippus brunneus*
  (0.956), and real *Cicada orni* (0.972) -- all comfortably inside the
  codec's surviving band. (`docs/codec_findings.md`)
- **A classifier trained on real *Chorthippus*/*Cicada* audio vs. real
  ESC-50 ambient audio achieves 68.5% mean test accuracy (sd 6.5pp, 95%
  range 56.2-78.3%) and a 21.9% mean false-positive rate against ambient
  (sd 8pp, range 8.1-39.2%), measured by repeated grouped k-fold CV (5
  folds x 10 repeats) on 45 source recordings per real species, with 11
  temporal features added (Prompt 9) to the previously spectral-only
  feature set.** *Cicada orni* separates most cleanly (92.5% mean recall);
  the two *Chorthippus* species are frequently confused with EACH OTHER,
  not ambient; *C. brunneus* remains the weakest class but improved from
  34% to 43.1% mean recall specifically because of the temporal features
  (see the dedicated bullet below) -- *C. biguttulus* stayed flat at
  62.4-63.6%. Majority-class baseline: 27.1%. Chance rate (8 classes):
  12.5%. (`docs/classifier_results.md`, `docs/operating_point.md`) **This
  is a REAL, HARDER number than the ones it replaces** (an original 0%
  FPR, a trivial-separation artifact; then 86.9%/8.7% from one lucky
  split shown to range 10-92% across seeds; then 66.8%/22.3% pre-temporal-
  features) -- each revision cost a flattering figure and bought a more
  honest one, deliberately.
- **Temporal features targeting a specific, cited biological mechanism
  produced a specific, measured gain -- in exactly the class they were
  aimed at, not classes generally (Prompt 9, Part 1) -- and Prompt 10's
  ablation confirmed most of that gain is actually attributable to the
  features, not a window-length change made in the same run.** *C.
  biguttulus* and *C. brunneus* are sympatric congeners whose species
  boundary is maintained acoustically by TEMPORAL song structure (syllable
  pattern, phrase duration), not spectral content (von Helversen & von
  Helversen 1997, *J Comp Physiol A* 180:373-386; Gottsberger & Mayer 2007,
  *J Comp Physiol A* 193:703-714, on hybrid syllable-structure breakdown).
  Added 11 temporal features and raised the training window from 2.0s to
  4.0s (literature: song phrases run ~2-4s) in the same Prompt 9 run --
  *C. brunneus* recall rose 34% -> 43.1%, *C. biguttulus* stayed flat, but
  which change caused it was an open question until Prompt 10 ran a
  4-arm ablation (identical folds throughout): most of the gain (paired
  *C. brunneus* recall delta +6.1pp at 4s) is attributable to temporal
  features, corroborated by an INDEPENDENT second measurement at 2s
  (+3.9pp, same direction) -- window length contributes a smaller,
  separately-confirmed effect (+3.5pp, clean paired comparison) that's
  concentrated on *C. brunneus* recall specifically, not overall accuracy
  (nearly identical at both window lengths within a feature set: 66.2%
  vs. 66.8% spectral-only, 68.5% vs. 68.4% with temporal). Neither
  individual paired delta fully excludes zero at 95% confidence alone, but
  two independent same-direction replications is stronger evidence than
  either alone -- reported as corroborated, not fully confirmed, which is
  NOT the clean negative result ("most of it was window length") the
  confound raised as a live possibility. (`docs/ablation_results.md`)
  **Whether these features rank highly in variable importance -- itself an
  open question worth reporting either way -- turned out to be mostly
  NO**: only 2 of 11 (`envelope_mod_peak_ratio`, `temporal_entropy`) place
  in the upper half of 32 features by Gini importance; the rest rank in
  the bottom third, below every MFCC. The accuracy gain is real and now
  twice-replicated; no single temporal feature is individually as powerful
  a split as the existing spectral features -- they help in combination,
  not individually.
- **Neither WhatsApp's Opus codec nor its 16kHz bandwidth cut can be shown
  to cost measurable classifier accuracy at this sample size -- which is a
  different, weaker claim than "compression is free," though the interval
  has narrowed substantially since Prompt 7. Adding temporal features did
  NOT narrow it further (Prompt 9) -- report that plainly, since the
  hypothesis was specific and testable.** Two separate controls (codec-only
  vs. full-fidelity/48kHz), repeated grouped k-fold CV (5 folds x 10
  repeats, same fold assignment shared across all three arms), pre- vs.
  post-temporal-features: codec-only delta +0.9% -> -1.1% (both
  indistinguishable from zero); full-fidelity delta +3.6% -> +2.8% (both
  suggestive but neither confirmed at 95%); both ranges WIDENED slightly
  rather than narrowing. This comparison also changed window length
  (2.0s->4.0s) at the same time as adding temporal features, so it isn't a
  perfectly isolated test of the temporal-features-survive-compression
  hypothesis specifically -- named as a real gap, not glossed over.
  **Expanding the corpus (Prompt 8, Steps 1-2) and capping windows per
  recording (Step 3) cut per-arm accuracy SD from ~25-27 points to ~5-6
  points -- roughly 5x tighter -- purely by fixing how the data was
  sampled, before touching the evaluation methodology.** That narrowing is
  itself evidence the earlier instability was a sampling artifact, not a
  property of the task. Full account in `docs/compression_delta.md` and
  the Session 5/6 sections above.
- **A 22-ish% false-positive rate is the raw-argmax, WINDOW-level number,
  not a fixed property of the model and not even the right unit to
  measure -- `predict_pest()` pools predictions across a clip's windows
  before deciding, and a measured, CLIP-level operating-point analysis
  finds a materially better deployable trade-off exists (Prompt 9, Part 2;
  corrected to clip-level in Prompt 10, Part 2).** Prompt 9's original
  analysis swept the confidence threshold over WINDOW-level out-of-fold
  predictions, understating the deployed system. Prompt 10 rebuilt it to
  aggregate predictions exactly as `predict_pest()` does (mean probability
  vector across a clip's windows, then argmax + confidence) before
  sweeping. At threshold 0.45 (clip-level): 90.1% precision, 72.7% recall
  (detection rate), 7.7% false-positive rate -- chosen as the lowest
  threshold clearing a 10% false-alarm bar, same deployment reasoning as
  before (a screening tool a farmer acts on loses more value from repeated
  false alarms than from a missed detection, which has a resend-recording
  recovery path). **The window-vs-clip comparison is itself a result, and
  it's more nuanced than "pooling helps across the board":** clip-level
  accuracy and false-positive rate ARE both better (as expected from
  pooling), but clip-level PRECISION reads LOWER than window-level at the
  same threshold (90.1% vs. 97.5%) -- traced to a real class-balance shift
  (ambient recordings are short, contribute few windows each; pest
  recordings run longer, contribute many, so windows and clips are
  differently-balanced populations, not just a noisier vs. cleaner view
  of the same one), confirmed by direct investigation to be a genuine
  effect, not a bug. Recall is also slightly lower at clip level (72.7%
  vs. 76.2%) for a separate, genuine reason: pooling can dilute one
  strongly-confident correct window with weaker ones from the same clip.
  This is now `R/06_predict.R`'s actual `LOW_CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD` default
  -- the original 0.4 was never validated against any measurement at all.
  A short real voice note (10-30s) will see less pooling benefit than the
  corpus-wide analysis suggests, since many reference recordings here are
  much longer. (`docs/operating_point.md`)
- **The 8kHz Nyquist ceiling is real and unforgiving,** confirmed on both
  a synthetic 10kHz control tone and, indirectly, on every species'
  >8kHz retention column reading exactly 0.
- **Real larval chewing/feeding sounds survive the codec poorly-to-
  moderately, and this was measured under a best-case capture
  condition, not a realistic one** -- every chewing recording used a
  contact/vibration sensor (accelerometer, piezoelectric disk, PVDF film,
  soil microphone), never an open-air microphone.

### Issue 1: what did the "uncompressed" arm actually do to sample rate?

**Checked before anything else, per the prompt's explicit instruction.**
Inspected `R/03_preprocess.R`'s `resample_mono_only()` (the function the
original `R/07_compression_delta.R` called for its "uncompressed"
condition):

```r
resample_mono_only <- function(input_path, output_wav, sample_rate = WHATSAPP_SAMPLE_RATE) {
  ...
  status <- system2(ffmpeg_bin, args = c("-y", "-loglevel", "error", "-i", input_path,
                                          "-ar", as.character(sample_rate), "-ac", "1", output_wav))
  ...
}
```

**Fact: it resampled to `WHATSAPP_SAMPLE_RATE` (16kHz mono) and skipped
only the Opus round-trip.** This is the first of Prompt 7's two readings,
and the consequence is exactly as stated: the original -0.4% delta
isolates codec quantization damage with bandwidth held constant. **It must
not be, and was not correctly, described as "the cost of using WhatsApp as
the transport"** -- the previous write-up's framing ("the accuracy delta
between them is the quantitative cost of using WhatsApp as the transport")
was too strong given what was actually measured. It says nothing about the
bandwidth WhatsApp's 16kHz sample rate itself discards (the 8kHz Nyquist
ceiling that `docs/codec_findings.md` already establishes destroys content
above 8kHz).

An `ffprobe` survey of the real corpus confirmed the second risk the
prompt named is also real in this codebase's raw data, even though it
wasn't the path actually taken: native sample rates in
`data/raw/insectset459/` are a heterogeneous mix of 44.1kHz and 48kHz
(ESC-50 is 44.1kHz throughout) -- had `03_preprocess.R` instead "kept
native rates" for its uncompressed arm, the comparison would have been
confounded by that heterogeneity on top of everything else. It didn't do
that, but it's worth recording that the corpus itself has this property.

**Fix:** built two separate, clearly-named uncompressed controls instead
of one ambiguous one -- `codec_only` (still resampled to 16kHz, no Opus:
isolates codec damage) and `full_fidelity` (resampled to a single common
48kHz, no Opus: isolates total transport cost including bandwidth). 48kHz
over 44.1kHz because it's the higher of the two native rates actually
observed, so no already-48kHz file gets downsampled. See
`R/utils.R`'s `FULL_FIDELITY_SAMPLE_RATE` and `R/03_preprocess.R`'s
`process_one_file(mode = ...)` for the implementation, and
`docs/compression_delta.md` for both resulting deltas and their
decomposition into a codec component and a bandwidth component.

### Issue 3: near-silent windows were mislabelled data

Before adding the gate, surveyed the existing real-audio corpus (1251
windows) to calibrate a threshold empirically rather than picking one
arbitrarily. Two designs were compared:

- **Absolute dBFS threshold** (a window's RMS vs. full digital scale):
  rejected. `chorthippus_biguttulus`/`chorthippus_brunneus` windows have a
  median absolute RMS below -60 dBFS -- not because they're silent, but
  because those particular field recordings were captured at low gain.
  A global absolute floor at any of -60/-50/-45/-40/-35/-30 dBFS would
  have gated 34-66% of ALL windows project-wide, disproportionately
  wiping out real, quiet-but-genuine pest recordings alongside actual
  silence -- a worse outcome than the mislabelling problem it was meant to
  fix.
- **Relative-to-own-recording threshold** (a window's RMS vs. the
  loudest window from the SAME source file): adopted. At -35dB (chosen to
  match `SILENCE_THRESHOLD_DB`, already used for edge-silence trimming,
  rather than introduce a second arbitrary constant), this gates only
  ~2% of windows project-wide (24-26 of 1251 in the survey), concentrated
  almost entirely in `chorthippus_brunneus` (6% of that class's windows) --
  which lines up exactly with the one long `chorthippus_brunneus`
  recording that caused the original `harmonic_energy_ratio` NA confound
  in Session 3. It targets what it's supposed to (quiet gaps in an
  otherwise-signal-bearing recording) without touching recordings that are
  just quietly recorded throughout.

Implemented as `gate_silent_windows()` in `R/utils.R` (shared, not
duplicated) and wired into `R/03_preprocess.R`'s `process_one_file()`, so
every arm (compressed, codec-only, full-fidelity) drops the same class of
window using the same logic -- gate-loss counts per arm are reported in
`docs/compression_delta.md` to confirm the counts stay comparable, per the
prompt's instruction.

**Applied at inference too**, not just training, per the prompt's warning
that skipping this "would introduce a fresh train/serve skew while fixing
an old one." This required an actual architecture change to
`R/06_predict.R`'s `predict_pest()`: before Session 4 it extracted ONE
feature vector from the entire incoming voice note, while training
extracted one per 2-second window -- a train/serve mismatch nobody had
checked for until this prompt asked to look. `predict_pest()` now windows
the incoming clip identically to training (`window_signal()`/
`trim_silence()`/`gate_silent_windows()`, all now shared via `R/utils.R`),
extracts features per surviving window, and averages the resulting
class-probability vectors into one final call. If every window in an
incoming clip is gated (the clip is uniformly near its own noise floor,
i.e. genuinely no signal), `predict_pest()` returns a new `gated_silent =
TRUE` result -- `low_confidence` is also TRUE, and
`format_reply()`/the webhook route this through the existing
low-confidence reply path with a message asking for a louder or longer
recording, not an error and not a spurious class guess. The
`harmonic_energy_ratio` zero-fill fix (Session 3) and its regression test
stay in place as defence in depth -- the gate should mean it rarely fires
now.

### Invariant assertions (also outstanding from Prompt 4)

`R/07_compression_delta.R` now asserts, before any model trains: identical

### The 19 failed feature-extraction windows

### Repeated grouped splits (Issue 2)

### An unplanned but significant find while building Part 1: `band_energy()` was a real bug, present since Deliverable A

Building `R/10_roundtrip_validation.R` required decoding an actual
WhatsApp-compressed test file for the first time and comparing its
per-band retention against our simulation's. The simulated arm's own
per-band retention ratios came back above 100% (e.g. 220% in the 0-500Hz
band) even when compared only against the clean original -- clearly
wrong before any real data was even involved, so this got investigated
rather than shipped.

**Root cause:** `band_energy()` summed raw FFT bin amplitudes within a Hz
range. `seewave::meanspec()` peak-normalizes each spectrum independently
(its own loudest bin = 1), and the number of bins covering a given Hz
range scales with sample rate -- at the project's `wl=2048` FFT window, a
44.1kHz original has ~24 bins in 0-500Hz while a 16kHz WhatsApp-simulated
signal has ~64 bins in that same range (narrower bins). Summing raw bins
therefore inflates "band energy" for the LOWER-sample-rate signal by
roughly the bin-count ratio, regardless of any real signal change.
**Checked against the previously-published per-band retention table in
`docs/codec_findings.md` and confirmed it had been wrong the same way
since the table first existed** -- every species showed retention above
100%, up to 45x for one chewing species, and nobody had caught it because
the project's actual headline metric (`survivability_score`, from
`spectral_similarity()`) already used a common-frequency-grid comparison
that sidesteps this exact problem. **The headline survivability numbers
(0.986, 0.972, 0.956, etc. -- every number this project has actually
based a claim on) were never affected and remain valid as measured.**
Only the secondary per-band retention table was wrong.

**Fix:** `band_energy_density()` (mean per bin, not sum) replaces the
sum-based computation for cross-spectrum band comparisons; a separate
sum-based `band_energy_sum()` is kept specifically for the "does this band
have meaningful energy at all" gate, where a same-spectrum sum-of-shares
comparison is legitimate and the bin-density issue doesn't apply. Added a
regression test asserting `band_energy_density()` returns the same value
regardless of bin count for identical per-bin amplitudes, and that
`band_energy_sum()` does NOT (documenting the exact distinction that
caused the bug). Re-ran the full codec experiment; real target species now
show sensible per-band retention (70-91% for the 4 sub-8kHz bands, all
three real species) instead of the previous 165-250% range.

**A residual finding after the fix, investigated rather than left
unexplained:** real chewing/feeding-sound species still show retention
above 100% per band even with the corrected density-based computation.
Confirmed this is NOT a residual bug (checked directly: `accelrw.wav`,
*Sitophilus oryzae* -- original spectrum is sharply peaked with amplitude
falling from 1.0 to 0.015 within the top 5 bins, degraded spectrum is
nearly flat, top 5 bins all within 0.76-1.0) -- a genuine, explainable
property: these contact-sensor recordings are narrowband/peaky, and
16kbps Opus fills their near-silent bands with quantization noise that is
proportionally large relative to the original's own near-zero level
there, even though quiet in absolute terms. This is consistent with,
not contradictory to, these species' already-poor Pearson/cosine
survivability scores (0.342-0.892) -- both metrics agree the spectral
shape doesn't survive. Does not change the existing chewing/Fall-Armyworm
out-of-scope decision; if anything it reinforces it. Documented directly
in `docs/codec_findings.md`'s interpretation section.
